Depending on exactly how it feels, there are a few different words for that experience.
If you are actively in the zone trying to create something, what you are experiencing is called **audiation**. Coined by music educator Edwin Gordon, audiation is essentially "hearing" and comprehending music in your head when no physical sound is playing. Just like *thinking* is to language, *audiating* is to music.
However, musicians and songwriters use a few different terms depending on how those musical parts are arriving:
### 1. Involuntary Musical Imagery (An "Earworm")
If a specific melody, rhythm, or bassline pops into your head out of nowhere and keeps looping, psychologists call this **Involuntary Musical Imagery (INMI)**. In casual conversation, we just call it an **earworm** or a **brainworm**. It feels like your brain's auditory cortex has a mind of its own and is playing a track on repeat.
### 2. Musical Inspiration or "A Flash of Genius"
When those several different parts—like a drum beat, a bassline, and a vocal melody—all click together simultaneously out of nowhere, songwriters often call it a **flash of inspiration** or **receiving a download**. It feels less like you are *writing* the song and more like you are *tuning into a radio station* that is already playing it.
### 3. Mental Arranging or Sketching
If you are intentionally organizing those pieces in your head—thinking about how the verse transitions into the chorus, or how the guitar part sits on top of the bass—you are **mentally arranging** or **internal sketching**. Many famous composers, like Mozart, famously wrote entire symphonies completely in their heads before ever touching a pen to paper.
> **Pro-Tip for Songwriters:** When this happens, it is incredibly fleeting! The human brain is terrible at holding onto multi-part musical ideas for long. The moment you hear those parts appear, hum the melody into a phone voice memo app, tap out the rhythm, or jot down the structure before it slips away.
