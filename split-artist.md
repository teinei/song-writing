This is a really common headache called an **artist profile split**. When you use a second distributor and just type in your artist name, their system often creates a brand-new page instead of linking to your original one because it doesn't automatically know you are the same person.
Don't worry, your data and releases aren't lost. You can fix this relatively quickly by merging the two profiles.
Here is exactly how to solve it step-by-step:
### Step 1: Decide which profile is your "Main" one
Look at both profiles on Spotify and grab their URLs or URIs (tap the three dots on the profile -> **Share** -> **Copy link to artist profile**).
 * **Profile A (Keep):** This should be the profile that has your verified blue checkmark, your followers, and your original music.
 * **Profile B (Merge/Remove):** This is the accidental duplicate profile that only has the music from the new distributor.
### Step 2: Request a profile merge
You have two different routes to request the merge. Doing both can speed up the process, but starting with Spotify directly is usually the fastest.
#### Route 1: Directly through Spotify for Artists (Fastest)
If you already have access to your main Spotify for Artists dashboard:
 1. Log in to **Spotify for Artists**.
 2. Go to the **Help / Support** section (usually a question mark icon or a menu at the bottom).
 3. Look for the **"Music mixed up with another artist"** or **"Content Mismatch"** form.
 4. Fill out the form explaining that your catalog is split across two profiles. Provide:
   * The link to your **Main Profile** (the one you want to keep).
   * The link to the **Duplicate Profile** (the one you want removed).
   * The links/URIs of the specific tracks or albums that need to be moved over to your main profile.
#### Route 2: Through your new distributor
Your new distributor has a direct metadata pipeline to Spotify and can submit a fix request for you.
 * Log into the new distributor’s dashboard.
 * Find their support section and look for **"Fix artist split"** or contact their support team directly.
 * Tell them: *"My latest release went to a duplicate Spotify profile. Please map this release to my existing Spotify Artist URI: [Insert your Main Profile Link here]."*
### Step 3: Prevent this from happening again
To ensure future releases from both distributors land on the correct page automatically, you need to link your **Spotify Artist URI** permanently into your new distributor's system.
Most major distributors (like DistroKid, TuneCore, CD Baby, etc.) have an account settings page or a specific field during the upload process called **"Spotify Artist ID"** or **"Existing Artist Page."** Copy the unique ID from your main profile link (the string of numbers/letters at the very end of the URL) and paste it into your new distributor's settings so they always point your music to the right house.
> **What to expect:** Once Spotify receives the merge request, it usually takes anywhere from **3 to 7 business days** for the systems to update. Your music will seamlessly slide over to your main profile, your play counts will stay intact, and the accidental duplicate profile will completely disappear.
